Just down the road

The San Juan River — World Class Water

The Cockhouse sits 5 miles from Navajo Lake and just 6 miles to the Quality Waters from one of the greatest trout fisheries on the planet. The San Juan River below Navajo Dam is a year-round tailwater fishery ranked as a Top-10 Fly Fishing Destination in the United States — right in your backyard when you stay with us.

Cold, clear water flows out of the bottom of Navajo Dam twelve months a year, creating stable temperatures that never rise above the mid-40s. The result is an extraordinary concentration of fish — up to 15,000 rainbow and brown trout per mile — with fish averaging 16 to 18 inches and trophy specimens well beyond that.

The famed Quality Waters section stretches 3.75 miles below the dam — flies and artificial lures only, single barbless hook, catch-and-release. Below that, year-round bait fishing with standard regulations. The river can be waded or floated, and fishes well in every season. Winter months are often spectacular.

Wild Out Pro's guided fly fishing trips put you on the San Juan with experienced local guides who know every run, pool, and hatch cycle. Float trips in a drift boat or wade the upper stretch that boats can't reach.

The land & the legend

A Place With History

From the Pit to the Lodge

Before it was a hunting lodge, this land had a different kind of fight in it. The Cockhouse sits on property that locals say was once used as a cockfighting arena — part of a long tradition across rural New Mexico that stretched back generations, woven into the culture of ranching communities and the high desert frontier.

Cockfighting was legal in New Mexico longer than almost anywhere else in the country. New Mexico was the 49th state to ban the practice, finally outlawing it in 2007 — decades after most of the country had moved on. In San Juan County, the sport was part of the rural fabric, held at local arenas where ranchers, oilfield workers, and their families gathered on weekends. The fighting rooster wasn't just a bird — he was a symbol of grit, toughness, and the unbroken spirit of the West.

When Derek Martin and Wild Out Pro took over this property, they honored that history with a name and a logo: a rooster with boxing gloves raised, standing in an archway. The Cockhouse. It wears its past without apology — and now it channels that same fighting spirit into the pursuit of trophy game.

Blanco, New Mexico

Blanco sits at 5,568 feet in the high desert of the Colorado Plateau, about 20 miles east of Farmington in San Juan County. The land here has been inhabited for over a thousand years — Ancestral Puebloans built great houses and kivas across this basin, and the ruins of Chaco Canyon lie just to the southeast.

Spanish settlers arrived in 1598, and by the late 1800s ranching families had spread across the high desert, carving out homesteads in the cedar and sage. Blanco's post office opened in 1901. The Rose of Lima Catholic Parish, established in 1900, was the first parish in all of San Juan County.

Then came oil. In 1911, pioneer Henry F. Brock struck oil while drilling for water — accidentally discovering what would reshape the entire region. By the 1920s, the Blanco-Mesaverde gas field was one of the most significant in the San Juan Basin, and the energy boom transformed sleepy desert communities into something entirely new.
